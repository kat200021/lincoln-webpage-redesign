What Is a Bunion?

A bunion forms when the big toe gradually shifts out of alignment, pressing toward the second toe instead of pointing straight ahead. Over time this pulls the bones of the forefoot out of position, creating the bony bump that shows on the side of the joint. Because the shift happens slowly, many patients don't notice symptoms until the bump has become fairly pronounced.

What Causes a Bunion?

Bunions typically run in families — it's the shape and mechanics of the foot that get passed down, not the bunion itself. Footwear that squeezes the toes, such as narrow shoes or high heels, doesn't create a bunion on its own, but it can speed up how quickly an existing tendency turns into a noticeable deformity.

Common Symptoms

  • Soreness or aching at the base of the big toe
  • Redness and swelling around the joint
  • A burning or warm sensation
  • Occasional numbness in the toe

Symptoms tend to flare with tight or narrow footwear and after long periods spent on your feet.

Diagnosis

A bunion is usually easy to spot on exam, but Dr. Hoffmann or Dr. Lagdaan may order X-rays to see how far the joint has shifted and plan the right course of treatment.

Non-Surgical Options

Most bunions can be managed without surgery, at least in their earlier stages. Options include:

  • Footwear changes — wider toe boxes, lower heels
  • Padding over the bunion to reduce friction and pressure
  • Activity adjustments to limit aggravating movements
  • Anti-inflammatory medication for pain and swelling
  • Icing to calm inflammation after activity
  • Custom orthotics to correct alignment and reduce strain

When Surgery Makes Sense

When conservative care no longer keeps pain manageable, surgical correction becomes worth discussing. The right procedure depends on the severity of the deformity, your activity level, and your goals — Dr. Hoffmann and Dr. Lagdaan will walk you through the options and expected recovery timeline for your specific case.
